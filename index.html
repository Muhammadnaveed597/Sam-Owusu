Your new About section

The short version of what changes

Your current About argues well for the system, but it is written entirely in the language of ideas. The new version keeps your measured, systems-first voice and puts your story in front: the Marine who learned that safety is built before failure, the finance and infrastructure background that taught you scalability, and the book that holds the philosophy. It also adds what is missing today: the concrete program, and a next step.

I learned preventive systems the hard way, before I ever used the word health. For five years in the Marine Corps, I supervised avionics and electrical systems on aircraft. Quality assurance. Ground safety. Collateral inspections. The core principle was simple and unforgiving: you do not wait for a failure to understand your system. You build the checks, the discipline, and the accountability before anything goes wrong. Later, in private equity and infrastructure development, I saw the same principle at a different scale. The assets that hold their value are the ones with maintenance built in. Deferred maintenance always gets paid, just at a worse price. Today I apply both lessons to one of the largest unsolved problems in healthcare: chronic disease in adults 40+. The current healthcare model is largely reactive. It intervenes after disease has progressed. I founded Salvitae to complement that system with proactive, structured intervention earlier in the health trajectory, where impact and cost-efficiency are highest. What we are building: A 12-week preventive health intervention model targeting the root drivers of chronic disease risk through behavioral, lifestyle, and data-informed protocols. Structured intervention models. Behavioral adherence systems. Preventive health education. Measurable outcome tracking across metabolic health, activity, and adherence. The distinction we build on: The longevity industry has plenty of measurement. Wearables, biomarkers, continuous monitors, AI interpretation. Measurement is not the outcome. Data only becomes valuable when it produces a better decision, and that decision becomes valuable when someone can sustain it. Salvitae works in that gap: connecting measurement to prioritization, action, adherence, and reassessment. The goal is a scalable model deployable across communities, employer populations, and health systems, improving healthspan while reducing long-term healthcare burden. I wrote The Forever Advantage about the philosophy behind this work: longevity as optionality, and the decisions that preserve tomorrow's choices. I am particularly interested in working with: Investors focused on health innovation and scalable platforms. Foundations advancing preventive health and population-level outcomes. Strategic partners building or deploying next-generation health models. If that describes you, or if you simply believe health deserves the same long-term planning as finance, message me here. I read every one.

A complete first post, written in your voice

In the Marine Corps, I supervised avionics systems where a missed reading could cost lives. We had instruments for everything. Hydraulic pressure. Fuel flow. Electrical load. Every system reported constantly. But nobody on the flight line believed the mission was accomplished because a gauge displayed a number. The number was the start of the work, not the end of it. A reading told you where to look. The inspection told you what it meant. The maintenance action, documented and verified, was what actually protected the aircraft. I think about that every time I see another health dashboard. Wearables that track sleep. Continuous monitors for glucose. Lab panels with forty biomarkers. Apps that aggregate it all into charts. Extraordinary instruments. And yet knowing your sleep is deteriorating does not improve your sleep. Seeing a concerning trend does not change the trend. A sophisticated health report does not change behavior by itself. On the flight line, we understood that data becomes valuable at exactly one moment: when it produces a verified action. Health has built the instruments. It has not yet built the flight line. The checks between the readings. The prioritization of what actually matters. The follow-through. The verification that the action worked. That is the layer we are building at Salvitae. Not another device. The discipline around all of them. Measurement is not the outcome. It never was. What is one health number you tracked this year that never turned into an action?
